# Themes

## Fate punishes interference

The [[The fakir|fakir]] made [[The monkey's paw]] "to show that fate ruled people's lives, and that those who interfered with it did so to their sorrow." Every wish is granted in a way that could be a coincidence, so the story never has to prove the magic. In **Morning**, Mr. White repeats Morris's warning: "the things happened so naturally that you might if you so wished attribute it to coincidence."

## Wanting what you already have

[[Mr. White]]: "It seems to me I've got all I want." The first wish isn't even his idea; [[Herbert White]] suggests it as a joke. The cost of the wish is exactly the thing he already had.

## Grief against reason

In Part III the roles flip. [[Mrs. White]], who laughed at the paw, demands the second wish. Mr. White, who believed in it, now calls it "a coincidence" because he's afraid it's true.

## Comedy first

Part I is nearly a comedy: bad chess, a henpecked joke, Herbert at the piano. The jokes set up the horror: "something horrible squatting up on top of the wardrobe" becomes the thing at the door.
