FADE IN:

EXT. LABURNAM VILLA - NIGHT

Rain sheets across a half-built road. Only two houses are lit. The gate of one bangs in the wind.

INT. LABURNAM VILLA - PARLOUR - NIGHT

A fire, drawn blinds, a chessboard. MR. WHITE (60s, thin grey beard) moves his king into danger. His son HERBERT (20s, quick, amused) studies the board. MRS. WHITE (60s, white-haired) knits by the fire and misses nothing.

MR. WHITE
Hark at the wind.

HERBERT
I'm listening.
(moves a piece)
Check.

MR. WHITE
I should hardly think that he'd come to-night.

HERBERT
Mate.

Mr. White throws up his hands.

MR. WHITE
That's the worst of living so far out. Pathway's a bog, and the road's a torrent.

MRS. WHITE
(soothing)
Never mind, dear. Perhaps you'll win the next one.

He catches a knowing glance between mother and son. The gate bangs. Heavy footsteps.

HERBERT
There he is.

Mr. White hurries out and returns with SERGEANT-MAJOR MORRIS (50s, burly, beady-eyed, face reddened by twenty-one years of India). Whiskey is poured. A copper kettle goes on the fire.

INT. LABURNAM VILLA - PARLOUR - LATER

The third glass. Morris talks, and the family leans in.

MR. WHITE
What was that you started telling me the other day about a monkey's paw or something, Morris?

MORRIS
Nothing. Leastways nothing worth hearing.

MRS. WHITE
Monkey's paw?

He fumbles in his pocket and lays it on the table: small, dried, dark as old leather. Mrs. White draws back. Herbert picks it up.

MORRIS
It had a spell put on it by an old fakir, a very holy man. He wanted to show that fate ruled people's lives, and that those who interfered with it did so to their sorrow. Three separate men could each have three wishes from it.

HERBERT
Well, why don't you have three, sir?

MORRIS
(quietly)
I have.

His blotchy face goes white. Nobody laughs now.

MORRIS (CONT'D)
The first man had his three wishes. I don't know what the first two were, but the third was for death. That's how I got the paw.

He takes it back and, without warning, throws it on the fire. Mr. White snatches it out.

MORRIS (CONT'D)
Better let it burn.

MR. WHITE
If you don't want it, Morris, give it to me.

MORRIS
I won't. If you keep it, don't blame me for what happens.
(beat)
Hold it up in your right hand and wish aloud. But I warn you of the consequences.

DISSOLVE TO:

INT. LABURNAM VILLA - PARLOUR - LATER

Morris is gone. Herbert sits at the piano, grinning.

HERBERT
Wish for two hundred pounds, then. That'll just do it.

Mr. White holds up the paw, embarrassed. Herbert strikes a grand chord.

MR. WHITE
I wish for two hundred pounds.

He cries out and drops it. It lies on the rug.

MR. WHITE (CONT'D)
It moved. As I wished, it twisted in my hand like a snake.

HERBERT
Well, I don't see the money, and I bet I never shall.

Alone later, Herbert watches the dying fire. A face forms in the coals, simian and horrible. He reaches for a glass of water and his hand closes on the paw instead.
