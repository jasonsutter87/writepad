# Writing toward a target

How **The Sexton's Bell** was written: find stories you love, measure them, blend them, and write toward the blend.

## 1. Pick the books

Three stories in the same uncanny register, all public domain:

- *The Monkey's Paw*, W. W. Jacobs (1902): third person, lots of dialogue, short scenes
- *The Signal-Man*, Charles Dickens (1866): long, rolling, first-person sentences
- *The Tell-Tale Heart*, Edgar Allan Poe (1843): short, frantic, first person

In **Library** they're measured (numbers only, never the text) and blended **40 / 30 / 30** into the target.

## 2. Draft without looking

Draft 1 of each chapter was written straight through, without looking at the numbers. It's still there: open a chapter and pick **Draft 1** under **Onion skin → Compare with**.

## 3. Measure, then revise

**Compare → Against your target**, and the strip under each chapter title, showed what the draft was doing differently:

| | Draft 1 | Revised | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chapter length (words) | 418 · 474 · 509 | 516 · 541 · 587 | 503–743 (middle half) |
| Dialogue | 13% | 21% | 24% |
| Flagged stretches | 10 | 1 | — |
| Median sentence (words) | 10 | 8 | 11 |

What changed:

- **Chapters 2 and 3 were too short and nearly silent** (6% and 2% dialogue). Abel's confession was one 75-word sentence of reported speech. It's now a conversation, which fixed the length, the dialogue and the longest flagged sentence at once.
- **Four sentences in a row opened with "I"** and three with "The". Rewritten.
- **Two very long sentences** in chapter 1 were split.

## 4. Use judgement

The revision keeps one flag on purpose: a 57-word sentence while Tom is digging. It's meant to feel breathless. Flags are prompts, not rules.

The revision also overshot. Splitting long sentences took the median from 10 words to 8, and the target is 11. That's the next pass: join some of the short ones back up. Watch the strip while you do it.

## Try it

Open **The Sexton's Bell**, pick a chapter, and compare the two takes. Then press **▶ Listen** on each. **A/B listen** plays them back to back, which is the fastest way to hear what the numbers mean.
