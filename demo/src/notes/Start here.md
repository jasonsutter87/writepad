# Start here

This vault is a tour of WRITEpad, built around one short story: *The Monkey's Paw* by W. W. Jacobs (1902, public domain). Everything here is plain files: markdown notes you could open in Obsidian, and a `.writepad/` folder for drafts and settings.

Shortcuts say ⌘; on Windows use Ctrl.

## 1. Read a manuscript in blocks

Open **Manuscripts → The Monkey's Paw**. The story is split into seven blocks (scenes). The outline on the left is the running order; drag a block to move it.

## 2. Hear it

Put the cursor anywhere and press **▶ Listen** (⌘↵). The sentence being read lights up. Names like Laburnam, Meggins and fakir are said properly because of the **Pronunciations** list; add your own.

First time on a new computer? Choose **Set up narration** when it asks. It installs the voices (about 350 MB).

## 3. Compare versions with the onion skin

Open **The first wish**. It has two versions: Jacobs's original and a "Modern pass". Pick the other one under **Onion skin → Compare with**: words you added are tinted, words that were cut show as ghosts. Slide **Ghost** to fade them. **A/B listen** plays one, then the other.

In the Modern pass, Mr. White's thought is a line in *italics*. Italic lines are read in a second voice.

## 4. See what the agents did

Two blocks have takes marked with a robot. They were made by AI agents working through WRITEpad's MCP server:

- **The visitor**: "tighter opening" by *line-editor*
- **The knocking**: "alt ending: the door opens" by *story-doctor*

Agents can only write their own takes. **★ Make live** is always your call.

## 5. Cuts

Under **Cuts**, apply **Jacobs 1902**, **Modern pass** or **Cold open**. A cut remembers the block order and which version is live. Cold open starts at the knocking.

## 6. The world

Open **World**:

- **Graph**: people, places, the paw, and how they connect. Names are found in the manuscript too, so [[Sergeant-Major Morris]] links to every scene he's in.
- **Timeline**: what happened in story order, from [[The fakir's spell]] to [[The empty road]]. The § chips show which scene tells each event. There's a branch at [[The knocking]] for the alternate ending.
- Two entries are **drafts** an agent proposed: [[Alt ending (the door opens)]] and [[The first owner]]. Accept them or delete them.

While you're writing, **In this scene** lists who and what each block names.

## 7. A screenplay and a table read

Open **The Monkey's Paw (screenplay)**. It's [Fountain](https://fountain.io): headings in bold, cues and dialogue indented. **Tab** cycles a line through action → character → parenthetical → transition → heading.

Press **▶ Listen** for a table read: every character has their own voice (see **Cast**), and the narrator reads the action.

## 8. Library: learn from books

Open **Library**. Three books are measured: the story itself, Jacobs's *Many Cargoes*, and a volume of Poe's tales. WRITEpad keeps only numbers, never the text. *Many Cargoes* and Poe are blended 60/40 into a **target**, and **Compare** shows the manuscript **Against your target**. While you write, the strip under a scene's title underlines stretches that drift from it.

Under **Scripts**, **Book to script** lines the screenplay up against the story: which scene draws on which part, what was cut, and which lines survived word for word.

## 9. History

History is on, so this vault is a git repository. Open **History** to see the vault being built step by step. On **The first wish**, click an older version to onion-skin against it, or **Restore as new take**.

## 10. Export

**Export** writes the live cut as EPUB, PDF, Markdown, plain text, or an MP3 audiobook narrated with your voices.
