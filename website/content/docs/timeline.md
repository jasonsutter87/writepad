The **Timeline** lists your story's events in the order they **happen**, which isn't always the order you **tell** them. Each event links to the scenes where the reader sees it, so flashbacks, cold opens and narrators who disagree are easy to spot.

Click **Timeline** on the World tab.

{{< shot "timeline" "The timeline list with events, scene chips and told-earlier flags" >}}

## Add events

- **+ Event at the start** adds the first event.
- **+** between two events adds one there.
- From a page, **+ Event here** (in *In this scene*) adds an event linked to that page.

Give it a **Name** and, if you like, a **When** in story time ("the next morning", "Fri Apr 26 2041, 8:14am").

Each event is a note in your vault, so you can open it and write what happens.

## Reorder

Drag an event onto another to move it right after that one.

## Where each event is told

The **§** chips on an event show the scenes that tell it. Click one to open the scene.

When an event is told out of order, it's flagged:

- **told earlier**: the telling gets to it before events that happen ahead of it. A cold open, or a hint of what's coming.
- **told later**: the telling gets to it after events that happen later. A flashback.

## Read against a telling

**Read against** picks which telling to compare with:

- **each scene where it is**: the scenes as they stand anywhere in the vault.
- **a manuscript, as it is now**: that book's current order.
- **cut: …**: one of that manuscript's saved [cuts](/docs/cuts/).

Events that telling doesn't include are marked **not in this telling**. Keep a cut per narrator and you can read the same timeline against each one and see exactly where they part ways.

## Story vs telling

Switch from **List** to **Story vs telling** for a chart: when each event happens on the left, when it's told on the right, with a line between. A story told in order draws straight, parallel lines. Every line that cuts across the others is something told out of order, in color.

{{< shot "story-vs-telling" "The Story vs telling chart with crossing lines for events told out of order" >}}

## More than one timeline

**+ New timeline** starts a separate thread: a parallel world, another era, a story within the story. Each timeline has its own **+ at the start**, and an event can branch from one in another timeline.
