A **cut** is a named recipe for the whole book:

1. **Which pages** are in it.
2. **What order** they go in.
3. **Which version** (★ live take) of each page it uses.

You might keep a cut of the original draft, one for each revision pass, a shorter cut for a contest with a word limit, or one per narrator. Switching between them takes one click, and none of them ever deletes anything.

The **Cuts** panel is on the right while a manuscript is open.

{{< shot "cuts" "The Cuts panel with four cuts, and the outline with two pages left out" >}}

## Save a cut

Arrange the book the way you want it: drag pages in the outline, leave pages out, and make the versions you want live. Then type a name under **Cuts** (*Name this cut*) and click **Save**.

The number beside each cut is how many pages it has. The cut that matches the book right now is highlighted.

## Apply a cut

Click a cut to apply it. The book switches to it:

- Its pages move into its order.
- Each page switches to the version the cut uses.
- Pages the cut doesn't use are **left out**. They move below a *Left out of this cut* line in the outline, struck through.

Click another cut to switch again. A cut that uses every page brings them all back.

## Leave a page out

Hover over a page in the outline and click **−** (*Leave out of this cut*). The page stays in the outline, struck through, and you can still open and edit it. It just isn't in the book for now:

- **Export** skips it, for every format including MP3 audio.
- The **word count** toward your target doesn't count it.

To put it back, click **+** on it, or drag it in among the pages above the *Left out of this cut* line. Dragging a page in among the left-out ones leaves it out.

{{< tip >}}
Leaving a page out never deletes it or any of its versions. It's the way to take a page out of the book while keeping it around.
{{< /tip >}}

## Delete a cut

Click **×** beside a cut, then click again to confirm. Only the recipe goes: your pages and their text are untouched. **Undo** in the message at the bottom of the window brings the cut back for a few seconds afterwards.

## Cuts and the timeline

On the [timeline](/docs/timeline/), you can read events against any saved cut. That shows where each event falls in that telling, and which ones it tells early or leaves out. A cut per narrator makes it easy to compare how each one tells the same story.
