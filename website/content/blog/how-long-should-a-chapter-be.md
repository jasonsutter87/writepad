Ask "how long should a chapter be?" and you'll get the same answer every time:
somewhere between 2,000 and 5,000 words, unless it isn't. That's true, and it
doesn't help you decide anything.

A better question is: **how long are the chapters in the books yours will sit
next to on the shelf?** That you can measure.

## Why the usual advice is so vague

Chapter length is a pacing tool, not a rule. Short chapters speed a reader up.
Each break is a small reward and an easy "one more". Long chapters slow a reader
down and let a scene breathe. Thrillers lean short. Literary fiction and epic
fantasy often run long. Middle-grade novels run short because the reader is
younger and the book is shorter.

So any single number is an average over books that want different things. The
range that matters is the one for *your* kind of book.

## Measure instead of guessing

Pick three to five published novels you'd be happy to be compared with: same
genre, similar audience, ideally ones you've loved reading. Then look at three
numbers for each:

1. **Words per chapter.** Not just the average. Look at the spread. Most books
   have a comfortable middle and a few outliers (a one-page chapter for a
   shock, a long one for a set piece).
2. **Median sentence length.** It tells you how the prose *feels*. Twelve
   words a sentence reads quick and plain; twenty-two reads looser and more
   lyrical.
3. **How much is dialogue.** A book that's 45% dialogue moves very differently
   from one that's 15%.

You can count by hand with a page sample, but it's slow and it's easy to fool
yourself with a sample of one chapter.

And if you already know your numbers ("I want chapters of 2,500 to 3,500
words"), you can type them in yourself. Your own numbers win over the blend.

## A few rules of thumb that do hold

- **End chapters on a question, not an answer.** Length matters less than
  where the break falls.
- **Vary it.** A run of identical lengths flattens the rhythm the same way
  identical sentence lengths do.
- **Check the opening chapters hardest.** Readers decide early. If your first
  three chapters are twice the length of your genre's norm, that's worth a look.
- **Revise to the range, not the number.** Aim for most chapters to land
  inside the comfortable middle of your comparison books. Let the outliers be
  outliers on purpose.

The right chapter length is the one that makes your reader turn the page. The
books you love already worked that out. Measure them.
